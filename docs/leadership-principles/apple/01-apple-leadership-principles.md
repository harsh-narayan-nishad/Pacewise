# Apple's Leadership Principles

Apple does not publish a numbered leadership list. It publishes a short set of values on its investor site, and those headings are used below. The text under each is original and describes what the value means when the product is Pacewise.

Reference: [Apple, Our Values (investor.apple.com/our_values)](https://investor.apple.com/our_values/)

## Accessibility

**In Pacewise:** every reader should be able to use the product, not only the average one.

- Controls work from the keyboard, carry clear labels, and keep visible focus.
- Motion can be reduced, text can be enlarged, and colour is never the only signal.
- Docs are written plainly so assistive tools read them cleanly.

**Question to ask:** who cannot use this screen today, and what is the smallest change that fixes it?

## Education

**In Pacewise:** the product exists to help people learn faster and keep what they learned.

- Quizzes follow reading so understanding is measured, not assumed.
- The docs teach the thinking behind decisions, with case studies and diagrams.
- Personal analytics show a learner their own progress over time.

**Question to ask:** will the reader know or do something new after this?

## Environment

**In Pacewise:** efficient software wastes less energy.

- Docs are bundled at build time, so pages need no server work per request.
- Images are vector where possible and loaded only when needed.
- Search runs over a small local index before any heavier service is considered.

**Question to ask:** can this be done with less computing, storage or network?

## Inclusion & Diversity

**In Pacewise:** people with different languages, backgrounds and reading habits should feel the product was made for them.

- Text is treated as Unicode, so Hindi, Hinglish and English are handled alike.
- Reading speed is compared with the person's own past, not with a norm.
- Examples in docs draw on varied situations.

**Question to ask:** whose experience is missing from this design?

## Privacy

**In Pacewise:** reading data belongs to the reader.

- Docs and analytics need a signed-in user, so nothing is collected anonymously.
- Time records stay behind repositories and can be cleared by the user.
- Only what a feature needs is stored.

**Question to ask:** do we need this data, and would the reader be comfortable seeing it listed?

## Racial Equity and Justice

**In Pacewise:** fairness is checked in the product, not only stated.

- Content and examples are reviewed for stereotypes before they ship.
- Search ranking avoids signals that could favour one group of readers over another.
- Feedback from underrepresented readers is gathered and acted on.

**Question to ask:** could this feature treat some readers worse, and how would we find out?

## Supply Chain Innovation

**In Pacewise:** the supply chain is software: packages, fonts, images and services that others made.

- Dependencies are kept few, pinned, and checked for licences and known problems.
- Third-party content is used only with clear rights; otherwise it is rewritten or removed.
- Each outside service has a fallback so one failure does not stop reading.

**Question to ask:** do I know who made this, under what terms, and what happens if it disappears?

*Headings follow the values Apple publishes at [investor.apple.com/our_values](https://investor.apple.com/our_values/). The explanations are original and specific to Pacewise; they are not an official statement of Apple.*
