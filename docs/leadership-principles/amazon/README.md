# Amazon's Leadership Principles

Amazon says it uses these principles every day, from discussing a new idea to deciding how to solve a problem, and interviews are built around them. This folder has one page for each of the 16 principles, written in plain words for interview preparation. Read the official wording on Amazon's own page: [https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles).

## The 16 principles

| # | Principle | In one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Customer Obsession](01-customer-obsession.md) | Start from what the customer needs and work backwards; keep their trust above everything, including your interest in competitors. |
| 2 | [Ownership](02-ownership.md) | Act like the owner of the whole outcome, think long term, and never treat a problem as someone else's. |
| 3 | [Invent and Simplify](03-invent-and-simplify.md) | Expect new ideas from your team and from outside it, and keep making things simpler, accepting that new ideas can be misunderstood for a while. |
| 4 | [Are Right, A Lot](04-are-right-a-lot.md) | Good judgment built on instinct that you actively test: seek other views and try to prove your own belief wrong. |
| 5 | [Learn and Be Curious](05-learn-and-be-curious.md) | Never finished learning; curious about new possibilities and willing to act on them. |
| 6 | [Hire and Develop the Best](06-hire-and-develop-the-best.md) | Raise the bar with every hire and promotion, spot exceptional people, and take coaching seriously. |
| 7 | [Insist on the Highest Standards](07-insist-on-the-highest-standards.md) | Keep standards that others may call unreasonable, keep raising them, and fix problems so they stay fixed. |
| 8 | [Think Big](08-think-big.md) | A small view limits results; set a bold direction that inspires others and look for new ways to serve customers. |
| 9 | [Bias for Action](09-bias-for-action.md) | Speed matters. Many decisions can be reversed, so do not over-study them; take calculated risks. |
| 10 | [Frugality](10-frugality.md) | Do more with less; limits breed resourcefulness, and there is no credit for a bigger team or budget. |
| 11 | [Earn Trust](11-earn-trust.md) | Listen closely, speak candidly, treat people with respect, be openly self-critical and measure yourself against the best. |
| 12 | [Dive Deep](12-dive-deep.md) | Work at every level, know the details, check often, and doubt a story when the numbers disagree with it. |
| 13 | [Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit](13-have-backbone-disagree-and-commit.md) | Challenge decisions respectfully even when it is uncomfortable, then commit fully once a decision is made. |
| 14 | [Deliver Results](14-deliver-results.md) | Focus on the inputs that matter, deliver on time with the right quality, and rise above setbacks. |
| 15 | [Strive to be Earth's Best Employer](15-strive-to-be-earth-s-best-employer.md) | Make the workplace safer, more productive, more diverse and fairer; lead with empathy and care about people's growth. |
| 16 | [Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility](16-success-and-scale-bring-broad-responsibility.md) | As an organisation grows its actions reach further; think about secondary effects on communities and the world and leave things better. |

## How to use these pages

1. Read the one-line meaning, then the questions.
2. Pick one real story per principle from your own work. One story can serve two or three principles, but do not reuse the same one for every answer.
3. Write each story as **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, **R**esult, with a number in the result.
4. Say "I", not "we", for the Action. Interviewers score what *you* did.
5. Practise out loud: about two minutes per story.
