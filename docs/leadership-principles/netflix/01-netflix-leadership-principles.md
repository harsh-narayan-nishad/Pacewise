# Netflix's Leadership Principles

Netflix publishes its culture as four core principles, with eight values inside the first one. The headings below follow that published structure. The text under each is original and describes what the idea means when the product is Pacewise.

Reference: [Netflix culture, jobs.netflix.com/culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture)

## The Dream Team

**In Pacewise:** a small group where every person is strong alone and better together.

- Contributors are chosen for the skill the product needs next (editor, search, analytics), not for how long they have been around.
- Reviews look at the whole body of work, not one missed bet.
- Kindness and respect are a hiring bar, whatever the skill level.

**Question to ask:** would I want this person on the next hard problem, and would they want me?

### Selflessness
The best idea wins, wherever it came from. Time spent unblocking a teammate counts as real work. *In Pacewise:* review comments explain the reason, and credit goes in the commit message.

### Judgment
Prefer the fix that still holds in six months. Decide with incomplete information, then check the result against data. *In Pacewise:* repositories sit between screens and storage so a real backend can replace browser storage later.

### Candor
Say the useful thing early and directly, and say it to the person. Own mistakes in public and share what was learned. *In Pacewise:* the duplicate-key build failure was written up with its cause and a guard.

### Creativity
Welcome strange ideas and keep trying until one works. *In Pacewise:* a speed wheel, a clock that counts past zero, and a search that reads images.

### Courage
Challenge the default when the evidence says so, and accept the chance of being wrong. *In Pacewise:* removing content with unclear rights instead of hoping nobody noticed.

### Inclusion
Notice your own bias and design against it. *In Pacewise:* themes, text size, reduced motion and plain-language docs.

### Curiosity
Be as interested in other people's ideas as your own, and honest about gaps. *In Pacewise:* every case study starts from a question, not from a conclusion.

### Resilience
Adapt quickly, decide without long delay, and take on the hard task. *In Pacewise:* a loader was revised through several rounds until it felt right.

## People Over Process

**In Pacewise:** give people the context to decide, then keep rules few and simple.

- Each decision has one owner who asks others for objections, decides, and then everyone commits.
- A short written design note replaces a long meeting.
- Rules exist only where the harm is real: licensing, privacy and data safety.

**Question to ask:** is this step protecting the reader, or only protecting us from a mistake that rarely happens?

## Uncomfortably Exciting

**In Pacewise:** ambitious goals are worth some discomfort.

- Experiments ship small, are measured, and are dropped when the numbers say so.
- The roadmap includes work the team has not done before, such as scanned-page search.
- Content choices respect different tastes and cultures and give readers control over what they see.

**Question to ask:** if this works, what changes for the reader, and what is the cheapest test?

## Great and Always Better

**In Pacewise:** today's version is never the finished one.

- Culture and docs are edited as the product grows, not kept as a monument.
- Every release has a short list of what is still weak.
- Each new contributor is expected to improve how the team works.

**Question to ask:** what would make this noticeably better next month, and who will own it?
